Experience

A timeline of my co-op placements — a snapshot of each company and what I worked on there.

Sept – Dec 2026

Engineering Design Student

Amphenol Canada Corp.

Current Markham, ON

Amphenol is one of the world’s largest interconnect manufacturers; its Canadian division designs and builds high-reliability connectors and motor jacks for aerospace, defence, and commercial systems.

3D CAD modelling and drafting of connector components in SolidWorks, producing detail and assembly drawings for manufacturing. I support new product design from concept through design review, revise geometry against manufacturability and assembly feedback, and keep released models, drawings, and documentation current through engineering change notices. I also support design testing and qualification, feeding findings back into design revisions.

Jan – Apr 2026

ARIEL Engineering Assistant

TRIUMF

Vancouver, BC

TRIUMF is Canada’s national particle accelerator laboratory; its ARIEL facility produces rare isotopes for fundamental physics research and medical isotope development.

Structural design and FEA for a radioactive gas pumping manifold. I designed a wall-mounted stainless-steel support frame in SolidWorks and sized its members and clamps through iterative ANSYS analysis, cutting deflection 25% while holding stress under 105 MPa. I produced ASME Y14.5 GD&T drawings for fabrication, managed BOMs and revisions in SolidWorks PDM, and worked with technicians in the shop through fabrication and install.

May – Aug 2024

Mechanical Engineering Co-op

BC Hydro — Site C Clean Energy Project

Fort St. John, BC

BC Hydro is British Columbia’s provincial electric utility; Site C is its hydroelectric dam on the Peace River and one of the largest capital infrastructure projects in the province.

P&ID review, field inspections, and design revisions on a live construction site. I redlined P&IDs and mechanical layouts against codes and standards to flag constructability issues, verified installations in the field and documented discrepancies, and turned RFIs and Field Change Forms into design revisions coordinated across mechanical, electrical, and civil teams.
